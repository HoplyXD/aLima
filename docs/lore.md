## The Timeline

### Era I — The Golden Harbor of Katagman · c. 1300s–1400s CE 🏛️

- **Katagman** (the precolonial name of present-day **Oton, Iloilo**, on Panay) was a wealthy
  coastal **trading port**, sitting between the Iloilo and Batiano rivers — one of the most
  important seaports of the late 1300s–early 1400s **CE**.
- Panay and Cebu sat on the **gold routes**; gold was panned from the islands' rivers and worked by
  local smiths (hammering, repoussé, chasing).
- **Elite burials included gold.** High-status dead were interred with gold ornaments and, rarely,
  **gold facial covers** (eye-mask, nose-disc) over the face.
- **The belief that anchors our whole story is real:** early Bisayans believed the **gold coverings
  protected the dead and that the brightness of gold drove away evil spirits** wishing to occupy the
  body. *(This documented belief is the seed of aLima's entire premise — see Era III.)*

> ⚠️ Naming note: use **"Golden Harbor of Katagman."** No source calls it a "Golden Kingdom" — that
> phrasing is fiction; "harbor/port" is historically defensible.

### Era II — Rediscovery · 1967 & 2023 🏛️

- **5 June 1967** — the **Oton Gold Death Mask** is excavated *in situ* at **Grave #6, Mediavilla
  property, Barangay San Antonio, Oton**, during National Museum excavations led by **Dr. Alfredo
  Evangelista** and **Dr. F. Landa Jocano** (four properties dug: Mediavilla, Cornejo, Planta,
  Blandura).
- The mask is **two pieces** — an eye-piece (13.3 × 2.5 cm) and a nose-piece (16.3 × 5.5 cm),
  **13.09 g** of thin hammered gold with repoussé decoration.
- Associated grave goods were **not gold** (trade ceramics from Vietnam/Thailand/China; iron, brass,
  copper implements; shell ornaments; glass and stone beads) — the mask is the only gold from Oton.
- It is a **National Cultural Treasure**; since **20 Jan 2023** it is displayed at the **National
  Museum Western Visayas** (Iloilo City), exhibit *"Ang Tamóng nga Bulawan."*

> ✅ This is the real object our Portal teaches about. The player never "finds" this exact museum
> piece — they find Yuyu's **replica** (Era III), and the Portal points to *this* real record.

### Era III — Yuyu the Chronographer · decades ago 🏛️→✦

*(Fiction built directly on the Era I belief.)*

- **Yuyu** — the player's **grand-uncle**, younger brother of the player's grandmother **Lola** — is
  a *Chronographer*: a scholar of the memories held inside objects. He keeps a journal bound in
  **Chronos Emulsion**, a substance that sits outside the flow of time. ✦
- Studying the Golden Harbor, Yuyu becomes convinced the old belief was literally true — that
  **worked gold wards a certain kind of spirit.** As proof-of-concept and safeguard, he commissions
  (or crafts) a **replica of the Oton Death Mask in real gold**. ✦
- Yuyu was **loved and trusted** across the barangay. Five people carry his mark and his lessons:
  **Nang Shine** (his first love), **Nong Lave** (her grandson, the artisan), **Ayla**'s father (the
  scrap-man he always paid fairly), **Sam** (the archeologist, his last dig partner), and **Mr.
  Maverick** (the buyer he trusted to deal honestly). ✦ *(Cast per [`story.md`](story.md) §3.)*

### Era IV — The Curse · "the day the week stopped" ✦

- On a final investigation at Katagman, Yuyu **disturbs a malevolent spirit with power over time** —
  an original fiction for aLima. *(The Era I belief already assumes hostile spirits the dead needed
  gold to repel; this is one such spirit, invented for the game. Its name/design is deferred to
  cultural review — do **not** attach an authentic folkloric name to it.)* ✦
- The spirit **curses the Chronos Emulsion journal**, corrupting a scholar's tool into a trap: the
  shop and its five surrounding days fold into a repeating **five-day loop**. Yuyu begins to fade
  out of time. ✦
- Fading, Yuyu does the one thing that can hide the warding gold from the spirit: he **breaks the
  gold mask replica into five shards** and entrusts **one shard to each of the five people he
  trusts** — "one of five; keep it; don't open it; don't give it away." Then he vanishes, leaving
  the cursed journal open on the workbench, its first page set with an **empty five-slot case**. ✦

> This is why the game's five "fragments" exist and why no character may simply hand one over
> (CLAUDE.md §4-B): each shard drifted, lost, into the town's junk stream years ago. Reuniting all
> five reforms the warding mask.

### Era V — The Loop · the game present ✦ + 🔓

The player inherits the shop and the journal. The week will not end until the case is filled.

**The daily/loop rhythm** *(see [`story.md`](story.md) §2):*
- Forage the scrapyard → hand scrap to **Ayla** → triage her delivery → **restore** (3D bench) →
  **scan & judge** → **decide** (sell / return / museum / journal) → evening. The clock runs in both
  spaces; the week resets on Day 5.
- Completing a **route** is the moment a holder finally tells the whole truth — and that telling lets
  the Chronos-bound journal begin to *listen* for their shard. The shard is **RELEASED** into the
  yard; the player tracks it by **Cultural Echoes** (Hum → Melody → Voice → Heartbeat), carries it
  in, **cleans then opens** it (the two-stage gate, §4-D), and it **SEATS** in the journal's case —
  permanent (§4-A/B/I).

**The two archives, and where the Portal fires** 🔓
- **The required secret artifact is the Oton Death Mask.** In-fiction the player is recovering the
  shards of Yuyu's gold replica *of that mask*; each discovery and the final assembly resolve to the
  real Oton mask's identity.
- **On each seating, the Portal unlocks a documented fact** — about the Oton mask, Katagman, or the
  real Visayan gold-and-spirits belief — into the player's digital museum. The **assembled mask**
  becomes the crowning **online-museum** record (Gold → API museum, §4-F).
- **Portal wording discipline:** the Portal states *"Precolonial Visayans **believed** gold warded
  off evil spirits,"* never *"gold wards spirits."* The story treats the warding as literally true;
  the Portal only reports the documented **belief**. This is the single most important line to keep
  honest.

### Era VI — The Morning That Never Came · finale ✦

- The fifth shard seats. The journal clears every un-cleared page at once, and the five shards lie
  loose in the case.
- The player plays the **final assembly** — joining the five shards back into the whole gold mask,
  each join using a skill a route taught (restraint, the honest seam, the freed mechanism, the
  mended photograph, the fair trade).
- The reformed **gold mask wards the time-spirit** — the documented belief made literal — and the
  **curse breaks.** Yuyu returns, translucent at the edges, mid-sentence. The clock stops for the
  first time in the game.
- Last playable beat: **open the shop door on Day 6** — Saturday, the morning that never came.
- **The Portal record for the assembled Master Artifact goes live** 🔓 — the private week becomes
  public memory. The real Oton Death Mask's history is now in the player's global museum.

## Real-vs-fiction ledger (quick reference)

**Keep as REAL (cite):** Katagman = old Oton; gold-trading port, 1300s–1400s CE; the Oton Death Mask
(1967, Evangelista & Jocano, 13.09 g, two pieces, National Cultural Treasure, now at NM Western
Visayas); Visayan elite gold burials; the belief that **gold warded evil spirits** from the dead;
Visayan goldworking (hammering, repoussé).

**Keep as FICTION (frame as story/legend, never on the Portal as fact):** Yuyu, Lola, the five
holders; the Chronos Emulsion journal; the **time-spirit and the curse**; the **gold replica** and
its five shards; "the Golden Harbor was cursed"; any claim that gold rings/ornaments were **excavated
with** the Oton mask *(they were not — the real grave held only the mask + non-gold goods)*.

## Sources (for the 🏛️ REAL lines)

- Victor Estrella, *The Death of Gold in Early Visayan Societies: Ethnohistoric Accounts and
  Archaeological Evidences*, **Hukay** Vol. 20 (UP Archaeological Studies Program) — the authoritative
  paper: Katagman/Oton, grave goods, dating, and the gold-in-burial belief.
- **National Museum of the Philippines** — Oton Gold Death Mask Gallery (NM Western Visayas); the
  "gold drives away evil spirits" framing and the 2023 exhibit.
- **VERA Files**, *Oton death mask: Celebrating the afterlife* — separates Oton facts from general
  burial practice (use to avoid conflating Cebu finds with Oton).
- Yodisphere, *The Oton Gold Death Mask of San Antonio, Oton, Iloilo* — Grave #6 / Mediavilla, the
  four excavated properties, dimensions.
- Pedro **Chirino (1604)** — early account: gold rings, chains, bracelets buried with high-rank dead
  (supports the *belief/practice*, not a specific Oton find).
- *Gold in early Philippine history* (Wikipedia) — general context **only**; it mixes regions, so
  cite the primary sources above for anything stated as fact.
